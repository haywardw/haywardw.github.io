  <h2>Personal Statement</h2>
  <h1>My Practice as a Filmmaker</h1>

  <div class="statement-section">
    <span class="statement-section-label">Influences</span>
    <p>My biggest influences and idols in filmmaking are Trent Reznor, Dennis Villeneuve, and P.T. Anderson. I appreciate their contributions to the filmmaking world because of their distinctly atmospheric style within their respective creative disciplines. Villeneuve, for example, has an excellent cinematographic eye that slows down an otherwise fast-paced movie. Consider the beautiful expansive desert shots in the <em>Dune</em> movies and how they contrast to the grittiness and intensity of <em>Sicario</em>. I also greatly appreciate how he handles Frank Herbert's religious allusions in the <em>Dune</em> movies. In a similar vein, P.T. Anderson is equally talented in worldbuilding, though I appreciate how his impeccable character writing contributes to the overall atmosphere of his works. His character arcs have incredibly ambiguous moral and social commentary, and he masterfully adapts them to a specific archetype. For example, Daniel Plainview in <em>There Will Be Blood</em> is a clear villain type whose progress is confounded by the aggressive preacher Eli Sunday. The otherwise ruthless oil prospector Plainview is given incredible dimensionality when Sunday forces him to put on a show of empathy in church, which ultimately resolves in Sunday's demise. Lastly, I am a huge fan of Trent Reznor, and by association, Atticus Ross. Trent Reznor's production on his solo work as <em>Nine Inch Nails</em> is genius, and it translates extremely well to the screen. In a film I made recently, I used some of his work for a soundtrack, and it tied the film together extremely well. Driving and subtle electronic music create tension and intrigue that wouldn't otherwise exist.</p>
  </div>

  <hr class="statement-divider">

  <div class="statement-section">
    <span class="statement-section-label">Style & Approach</span>
    <p>All of these influences directly affected how I approached each film. I am a sucker for wide-angle shots, and I was constantly suggesting this to my cinematographer on each project because I enjoy the sense of isolation it brings the viewer. I also use religious allusions consistently within my films. Regardless of if someone is religious, their lives are deeply affected by it in ways that they don't realize, and I try to bring this out in my films. The influence of religion in society cannot be understated, and I believe it would be a mistake for any filmmaker to be agnostic to it. I also wrote all my characters to have more internal conflict than external. A lot of my favorite movies, such as Damien Chazelle's <em>Whiplash</em> and Danny Boyle's <em>Trainspotting</em>, emphasize internal or mental struggles rather than an external conflict, and their impact on me has profoundly shaped my filmmaking approach.</p>
  </div>

  <hr class="statement-divider">

  <div class="statement-section">
    <span class="statement-section-label">Music & Emotion</span>
    <p>I am not a musician, but music is the creative medium that I enjoy most. Unlike films, I believe that music is more directly tied to the creative's emotions. This influence seeps into my work. In my writing and editing, I directly address the emotions of the characters of my films. For example, in <em>Gimghoul or Nothing</em>, I was judicious in my editing to ensure that the main character Camden's solace, frustration, and curiosity were overt. I made cuts in conversations that showed a lot of facial expressions, and I purposefully made shots where Camden is alone a lot longer than the scenes where he is with his love interest.</p>
  </div>

  <hr class="statement-divider">

  <div class="statement-section">
    <span class="statement-section-label">Collaboration</span>
    <p>I am also a team player and extremely coachable, though I retain confidence in my creative decisions. I can confidently say that my crew members for each film will say that I held up my responsibilities and had an influence on the end product of each film. Though I have my own style, I always respect the decisions of the director and storyboarders in achieving their creative visions above my own.</p>
  </div>
