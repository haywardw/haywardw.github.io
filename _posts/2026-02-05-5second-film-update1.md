#### Update 1 - Progress up to Sunday evening

At the end of last Thursday's class, I met Kat, Jacob, Colin, and Jake. We all agreed to make a film together, and split the roles according to our interests and abilities as follows:

- Jake: Captain and Producer
- Kat: Filmer and Cinematographer
- Jacob: Script Writer and Storyboarder
- Colin: Film Cutter and Editor
- William: Blogger and Tech Person

Following that initial meetup, we split up and brainstormed ideas for our first film. Going off the successes and failures we saw from the films in class, we had a baseline idea of what might work well given this project's constraints. The 5 second film must be direct, but should still give the audience something to think about after its conclusion.

The next day we met on a video call to discuss roles. Jacob and Colin came through with the strongest ideas; Jacob pitched a bad roommate scenario, and Colin talked about his idea for a misleading Zoom interview. The group was very receptive to Colin's idea, so his idea was ultimately chosen. Sorry, Jacob 😬.

The rest of the meeting was spent enthusiastically discussing specific edits, camera angles, set designs, etc. that we each envisioned. Although we all had own our ideas for what the film would look like, there was clearly a shared understanding of the overarching message we wanted this film to send. In general, the film will touch on the issues with faux appearances in an internet-driven society, but the finer details are still being <em>hammer</em>ed out. 

#### Update 2 - Further refinement and filming

Over the course of the week, we continued to think about and refine our idea. Dr. Hammer and another group's suggestions pushed us to think more critically about our storyboard, and we made several improvements based on his feedback and guiding questions.

The refined premise of our film is as follows: open to a Zoom interview with an interviewer telling the candidate, Achan, that he got the job. Achan is well put together and seems to be in a clean room, but there is a flickering light above him. The next shot cuts to a whole shot of Achan's room. We see it is dingy, dank, and that this is likely where Achan lives. There will be a few very brief shots of Achan's desk and watch. Hospital bills, books, a photo of his sick mother, and a nice watch will be shown. The next scene is outside of the room with a man yelling down a hallway looking for his watch.

We hope to create a commentary on how far a person will go to appear professional for personal gain, while also touching on trust and materialism in the internet era. We want the viewer to feel sympathy for Achan's family and living situation, but we also want them to be shocked when it is revealed that he stole the watch. 

This exact idea wasn't what we originally arrived out. It started out a lot rougher, but we nailed down ideas regarding camera angles, lighting, etc. as the work went on. This will be apparent in our final cut. 

These ideas were further refined in a meeting we had the night before filming. We discussed props, logistics, actors, and everything else that would be necessary for a productive and fun filming session.

<em>Filming!</em>

The time has come. It's time to film!

Colin and I arrived about 30 minutes early to our set--a lab storage room in the bottom of Phillips Hall. The physicists that use the storage room for their research had moved a lot of stuff around since Colin had been there, so we had to rearrange the room a little bit before getting it to an apt state for setting up props.

Colin brought several trophies, faked medical bills, and a photo of his AI mom sick in the hospital. I brought some math related books to make Achan appear smart, and Jake brought some bedding to show that Achan was living in the decrepit lab.

After setting up the scene, the rest of the group arrived. We set up our mics and gimbals, and immediately began dicussing how each shot would be achieved.

We very quickly ran into an issue pertaining with the audio; there was a <em>loud</em> exhaust fan in the storage room that we could not turn off. Unfortunately, the microphone we picked up from the media library could not filter it out, so we resolved to filtering it out in post-production. 

Colin and I then changed into our costumes-- me, a dress shirt as I was playing the recruiter, and Colin, a nice white shirt with a tie on top and sweatpants with no shoes or socks on the bottom.

We were ready to act.

I huddled up in a closet with good lighting around the corner and hopped on a Zoom call with Colin for the first shot. We took this shot from two angles. One, a screen recording from my computer, and two, Kat filming a side view of Colin talking. That was our first major shot. Next, we took a sweeping shot out of the storage room to show the entire hallway, and then got our final shot of Jake, the man looking for his watch.

Our final shot was a lot of fun. We had multiple takes because we kept laughing, and we kept tweaking the way Jake walked down the hallway until we got something we all felt good about.

We took a lot of takes for each scene until we felt we had enough to chop up in post production, and then we called it a night.
